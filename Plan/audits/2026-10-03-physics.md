# Audit: physics integration (2026-10-03)

**Scope.** `Plan/physics.md` (W1–W4 and the post-W4 external-game requests) and
`Plan/physics-collision-layers.md` checked against the code at rev `42d56b95`. Files:
`crates/ludion-physics/src/{lib,systems,world,queries,impulse,joints,contacts,time_scale,meshes,pose}.rs`
and its `tests/` (pile, rebuild, live_edit, impulse and time_scale read closely, the rest skimmed);
on the engine side, `crates/ludion-engine/src/{scene_physics,collider_mesh,ragdoll,snapshot}.rs`, the
physics wiring in `engine.rs` (`build_schedule`, `drain_diagnostics`), `Transform`'s `PhysicsPose` impl in
`components.rs`, the physics checks in `scene_check.rs` and `hierarchy.rs`; `crates/ludion-spatial/src/spatial.rs`
(`rebuild`'s skip); `scripts/physics-features.mjs`; `vendor/rapier3d` and `vendor/parry3d` diffed against the
crates.io sources; `docs/physics.md`, `dev-docs/ludion-physics.md`, `dev-docs/physics-pin.md`.
`character.rs` was skimmed: it loads `kind: character` documents and has nothing to do with physics.
`games/vigil` does not use `ludion-physics`: none of its scenes has a `physics:` block, and its colliders
are all in the 2D spatial tier. The work was static reading plus two throwaway programs outside the repo,
built against `ludion-physics` by path and deleted afterwards. The keep-world reparent math in
`hierarchy.rs` is covered by `2026-10-03-hierarchy-prefabs.md` and is not reported again here.

## Overall assessment

The wrapper is well built. Bodies are created, rebuilt and retired in ascending entity order. Every
output stream (contacts, joint breaks, blast hits, query hits) is sorted by the wrapper's own key, never
by rapier's traversal order. The snapshot carries the creation-order tables, the touching set and the
impulse windows. The feature gate and the vendored-patch path checks hold, and I found no reachable
std-libm or `HashMap` in our own physics code. Two determinism-adjacent bugs survived: a snapshot taken
on a frozen tick loses `fresh_colliders`, so a resumed run blasts a body the uninterrupted run skips (a
throwaway program reproduced this); and a rebuild of a rigid body with no `Velocity` component throws
away its momentum, which `docs/physics.md` says does not happen (also reproduced). The rest are gaps
in error reporting and the docs. A rigid body or 3D collider in a scene with no `physics:` block does
nothing and reports nothing, and a moved `static` body never reaches the solver. The vendored rapier
patch now touches four files, while the plan and the devdoc still say one.

## High

None.

## Medium

### P1 — A snapshot taken on a frozen tick drops `fresh_colliders`, so the resumed run's next blast hits a body the uninterrupted run skips · Medium · [verified]

**Evidence.** `fresh_colliders` (`crates/ludion-physics/src/world.rs:412-415`) lists colliders inserted
since the last real step. Rapier resolves a BVH leaf by slot and ignores the generation
(`vendor/rapier3d/src/pipeline/query_pipeline.rs:298,386,428,552`, `get_unknown_gen`), so
`blast_targets` skips these colliders (`impulse.rs:335-339`). A frozen step returns before
`fresh_colliders.clear()` (`world.rs:1092-1096`), so the list carries over the frozen frame. `to_bytes`
refuses only a *pending* freeze (`world.rs:1019`). A snapshot taken after the frozen step is accepted,
it does not carry `fresh_colliders` (`SnapshotRef`, `world.rs:294-305`), and `from_bytes` starts the
list empty (`world.rs:1073`).

**Reproduction.** One rigid sphere, zero gravity. Tick 5 calls `freeze()` and edits the sphere's
`mass`, so the body is rebuilt and the new collider reuses the old slot. After tick 5, world B is
round-tripped through `to_bytes`/`from_bytes`. Both worlds then queue the same `radial_impulse` and
run tick 6:
- uninterrupted: `blast_hits = []`, and the sphere stays at `x = 1.0`;
- restored: one `BlastHit` with impulse `[3,0,0]`, and the sphere drifts to `x = 1.1` by tick 9.

**Why it matters.** Gate 2 (snapshot resume) is the plan's only protection for a resumed physics
session. `ludion dev` restarts and `engine.restore` go through this path, and a diverged keyframe
replay is the "flaky is P0" category. The same mechanism also explains a silent oddity in the
uninterrupted run: a body rebuilt or created into a reused slot is immune to blasts until the next real
step. Under a multi-tick hit-stop, that is the whole freeze. `docs/physics.md` warns only about a
teleport on the same tick ("do not blast a body on the tick you teleport it"); it says nothing about a
rebuild or a freeze.

**Fix.** Add `fresh_colliders` to the snapshot. This changes the blob format, so the keyframes would be
re-recorded, the same as for the impulse change. The other option is a sharper guard: drop a candidate
when its BVH leaf's AABB does not contain the collider's current AABB, which needs no state at all.
Either way, add a test that resumes in the middle of a freeze with a blast.

### P2 — Rebuilding a rigid body with no `Velocity` component throws away its momentum · Medium · [verified]

**Evidence.** `systems.rs:361-366` passes the ECS `Velocity`, or `None`, to `rebuild`.
`rebuild` → `remove_entry` + `create` (`world.rs:1557-1568`), and `create` sets `linvel`/`angvel` only
when `velocity` is `Some` (`world.rs:1420-1424`). Otherwise the new body starts at rest.
`docs/physics.md:57` says "A rebuild replaces the body at its current pose and velocity", and
`docs/physics.md:72` tells authors that a body moved by impulses needs "no `Velocity` component".

**Reproduction.** A sphere with no `Velocity` falls for 30 ticks and moves −0.0835 m per tick.
Editing its `mass` to 2.0 drops the next tick's displacement to −0.0017 m: it restarts from rest.

**Why it matters.** Every edit outside the in-place set rebuilds the body: shape, body kind, `mass`,
`lock` and `layer`. Changing a thrown prop's `layer` in flight ("becomes debris") is a common gameplay
pattern, and here it stops the prop dead, silently and repeatably. That is the hardest kind of physics
bug for an agent to diagnose from a trace.

**Fix.** When the ECS velocity is `None`, have `rebuild` read the old body's `linvel()`/`angvel()`
before `remove_entry` and pass them to `create`. Add a test next to `a_mass_edit_still_rebuilds`.

### P3 — A rigid body or 3D collider in a scene with no `physics:` block does nothing and reports nothing · Medium · [verified]

**Evidence.** The triple is installed only when the scene has a `physics:` block
(`scene_physics.rs:56-60`, `engine.rs:726`). `spatial::rebuild` skips every collider that physics
would claim (`crates/ludion-spatial/src/spatial.rs:569,582`). So a `body: rigid` or a `box`/`sphere`/…
collider authored without the block is owned by neither tier. Nothing reports it:
`scene_layer_diagnostics` and `scene_hierarchy_body_diagnostics` (`scene_check.rs:180,236`) cover
layers and parents only, and `drain_diagnostics` (`engine.rs:1080-1091`) has no such check. The
precedent exists one line away: `path_follow::report_uninstalled` (`path_follow.rs:205`,
`L`-coded) reports a `PathFollower` whose system is not installed.

**Why it matters.** Errors are the UX budget. An agent that pastes a rigid-body snippet into a scene
without the block gets a body that hangs in the air, with no message to iterate against.
`docs/physics.md:13` documents the opt-in, but nothing enforces it.

**Fix.** Add a `check-scene` diagnostic (a new `L####`, with a pointer to the collider and the help
"add `physics: { space: free }`"), plus a runtime latch report on the `report_uninstalled` model for
colliders spawned by game code.

### P4 — Moving a `static` solver body (protocol `component.set`, an editor gizmo or game code) never reaches the solver · Medium · [verified]

**Evidence.** `PhysicsWorld::push` does nothing for `BodyKind::Fixed` (`world.rs:1635`). `is_stale`
compares only the `Collider` and the mesh hash (`world.rs:1323-1331`), so a `Transform` edit on a
static body neither re-poses it nor rebuilds it. The renderer and the ECS show the wall at its new
place, and contacts and queries still use the old one, until a reload, which then places it
correctly. `docs/physics.md:130` documents this ("A `static` body's pose is read at creation only"), but
no diagnostic reports it.

**Why it matters.** The ECS is supposed to own authored state (`Plan/physics.md` §Why it is
compatible). A designer dragging a wall in the editor during a physics session, or an agent fixing a
misplaced ramp with `component.set`, gets a live session that disagrees with its own reload. That
costs autonomous iterations.

**Fix.** Keep the creation pose on `BodyEntry` and, when the ECS pose differs, call `set_position`
on the fixed body and wake its touching set (the `retune_in_place` wake). This is cheap: one compare
per static body per tick, and static bodies are few. At minimum, report a latched diagnostic the
first time a static body's pose diverges from its body.

## Low

### P5 — The vendored rapier patch now changes four files, but the plan and the pin devdoc still say one · Low · [verified]

`vendor/rapier3d/LUDION-PATCH.md` and the `.diff` cover `joint_constraint_helper.rs`, `collider.rs`,
`collider_components.rs` (a `ColliderChanges::MATERIAL` fix so a material edit on a resting pair is
re-read) and `Cargo.toml`. A diff against the registry's 0.35.3 matches exactly that. But
`dev-docs/physics-pin.md:39` says "byte-identical except one file", and the `Plan/physics.md` Status
entry for external-game request 0018 says "byte-identical but for `joint_constraint_helper.rs`". No Status entry
records the material patch at all. A pin port that follows the devdoc would miss two hunks. The
ritual applies the whole `.diff`, so this is a documentation risk, not a build risk. **Fix:** update
`physics-pin.md` and add a Status line for the material patch.

### P6 — A heightfield on a rigid body is accepted, though a mesh on a rigid body is refused for having no interior · Low · [verified]

`L1211` refuses `mesh` + `rigid` because "a triangle mesh has no interior"
(`crates/ludion-spatial/src/collider.rs:576-580`, `world.rs:1360-1369`). A heightfield has no
interior either: parry returns zero mass properties for it (`vendor/parry3d/src/shape/shape.rs`, the
`HeightField` impl), and `ColliderMassProps::Mass` then gives the body its authored `mass` with zero
inverse inertia (`vendor/rapier3d/src/geometry/collider_components.rs:132-136`). The result is a falling
terrain tile that can never rotate. **Fix:** extend `L1211` (or a sibling code) to `heightfield` on
`rigid`.

### P7 — The resolved-feature gate does not cover glam/glamx, which do the solver's vector arithmetic · Low · [verified]

`scripts/physics-features.mjs` checks the exact feature sets of `rapier3d` and `parry3d` and that rayon
is unreachable. It does not check `glamx` or `glam`. parry's `Vector`/`Rotation` are glam types
(`vendor/parry3d/src/math/mod.rs:67,97`). Glam features such as `fast-math` or `core-simd` would change
SIMD rounding in every pose product, and cargo unifies features across the workspace. The cross-arch
traces would catch a change, but only after a full two-machine run. **Fix:** add the expected `glamx`
and `glam` feature sets to the gate, with an explicit deny of `fast-math`/`core-simd`.

### P8 — `collect_contacts` computes a contact point for every touching pair on every step, sleeping pairs included, and keeps it only on a `Started` · Low · [verified]

`world.rs:1157-1199` runs `contact_point` (manifold walk and pose products) and `engaged` for every
active pair, then sorts them all. Only the `Greater` arm (a new pair) uses `point`/`normal`; the
`Equal` arm reads only `impulse`. In a resting pile that is O(all contacts) of wasted work per tick
inside `physics_step`'s measured cost. **Fix:** first collect `(a, b, impulse, pair index)`, then compute
the point only for pairs that are absent from `touching`. Re-measure the stress budget afterwards; the
budget may need tightening.

### P9 — The struct docs promise that "a rapier set is never iterated", which the code no longer keeps · Low · [verified]

`PhysicsWorld`'s docs (`world.rs:330-335`) and the plan's §Why state that the handle table is the
only iteration order and that a rapier set is never iterated. `record_detection_poses` iterates
`rapier.colliders` (`world.rs:1121`), and `collect_contacts` iterates `narrow_phase.contact_pairs()`
(`world.rs:1157-1160`). Both are order-insensitive: one fills a buffer indexed by slot, the other sorts
afterwards. So nothing is wrong today. The stated invariant, though, is what a later maintainer will
rely on. **Fix:** reword it as "nothing whose order reaches an output iterates a rapier set unsorted",
and name the two order-insensitive walks.

### P10 — Teleporting a kinematic body is undocumented, and rapier turns it into a velocity · Low · [suspected]

`push` always sends a kinematic body's pose with `set_next_kinematic_position` (`world.rs:1636-1638`).
For a position-based kinematic body, rapier derives the body's velocity from the pose delta over `dt`,
so snapping a moving platform or a door back to its start launches whatever touches it.
`docs/physics.md`'s "Teleports" paragraph covers only rigid bodies. **Fix:** document this, and if a
game asks for it, add a `teleport` that also calls `set_position`.

### P11 — The tier split is one predicate written three times · Low · [verified]

`ludion_physics::claims` (`systems.rs:244-246`) and the two skips in `spatial::rebuild`
(`spatial.rs:569,582`) are the same expression. The "exact complement" guarantee in `claims`'s docs
holds only by convention: `ludion-spatial` cannot call into `ludion-physics`. **Fix:** move the
predicate onto `Collider` in `ludion-spatial` (for example `Collider::is_solver_owned`) and have both
tiers call it.

## Checked and healthy

- **Creation order and handle reuse.** `write` joins in ascending entity order. Creates, rebuilds and
  retirements apply in that order, and rapier's free lists are serialized with the arenas, so slot reuse
  is a pure function of the operation sequence. `rows`/`joint_rows` are rebuilt from the tables after
  a restore, never by walking a rapier set.
- **Output ordering.** Contacts are sorted by `(a, b)` and merge-diffed against a sorted `touching`.
  The `Started` point uses a total-order pick key. Joint breaks are sorted by carrier, blast hits by
  entity within each blast, query hits by `(distance, entity)` and overlaps by entity.
- **Snapshot coverage.** The table (with `source` sent through JSON to avoid bincode's
  `skip_serializing_if` desync), `mirrored` baselines, `touching`, the impulse windows, joints and their
  events, and blast hits are all captured. Pending impulses, time scales and freezes are refused rather
  than dropped. Resume tests exist for the pile, contacts, joints, live edits, gravity scale, compound
  and time scale.
- **Edit detection.** `rebase_mirrored` compares against the bits a lossy `Transform` reads back, so a
  resting body sleeps and an untouched component is never mistaken for a teleport. Kinematic pushes
  wake only when the pose changes (`set_next_kinematic_position` compares against the current pose).
- **Despawn, component removal and claim changes** retire the body (and so its joints, through rapier's
  cascade). `reconcile_joints` reattaches on a vanished handle and reports `L3035` only on the
  transition into the inert state.
- **Determinism hygiene.** No std transcendentals in the crate. `to_radians`, `normalize` on a scalar
  `Vec3` and `sqrt` are correctly rounded, and the crate's denylist `clippy.toml` is present. The
  feature gate pins `enhanced-determinism` without `parallel`, and both vendored crates are verified as
  path packages. The vendored trees match the registry sources plus exactly the stated diffs.
- **Hierarchy.** Rigid and static solver bodies under a `Parent` are dropped and reported
  (`L1221`/`L1222`) at check time, at reparent time and at runtime. A kinematic body is posed from its
  world pose, with `L1223` on shear. Physics adds no transform composition of its own beyond
  `derived_world_pose`.
- **Collision layers.** Symmetric masks with the `And` test. A table change re-applies groups in table
  order and retries only layer rejections. A restored world's first `apply_settings` finds identical
  groups and wakes nothing.
- **A self-joint (`a == b`) inserted directly by game code** is a harmless no-op in the solver (probed:
  the body free-falls unaffected). The validator refuses it on the scene and protocol paths.
- **Gates.** The trace tests bless only under `LUDION_BLESS`, check the line count, and pair the
  byte-identity check with a distinct-line floor, so a sleeping pile cannot pass vacuously.

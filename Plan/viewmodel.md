# Plan: Viewmodel — a first-person held-object layer in the main scene pass

> Status: **in progress** — components and codes shipped (subtask 1a); extraction and rendering follow. An external game's request 0003 (priority *blocking*). Code facts read from the tree on 2026-11-07 (file:line pointers are of that date; re-grep before editing).

## Context

A first-person game draws a held gun that must read clearly at all times: it never clips into a wall when fired point-blank, it keeps a fixed apparent size under FOV kick, and it is lit by the scene with emissive parts that bloom. The engine has one scene camera (a second is `L2101`), one depth buffer in standard [0,1] depth, no per-camera render layers, and no depth-range control. Every workaround fails:

- **The gun parented to the camera at world depth** pokes through walls and scales with the gameplay FOV.
- **A scaled-down gun near the eye** breaks lighting, shadows and perspective.
- **The overlay pass** is UI and debug only and cannot light scene meshes.
- **A gun at a faked depth** leaves SSAO and SSR halos on the world around it, and the wall it pokes into shadows it.

So the engine needs a viewmodel layer: a per-entity flag, an own projection and a reserved near depth slice in the same pass, shadow and post rules that keep the gun readable, and a camera source that cannot swim against the world at high refresh rates.

## Decisions

1. **A flag plus an optional lens.** `Viewmodel` is a field-less flag, authored `Viewmodel: {}`, inherited down `Parent`/`ParentJoint` by **OR** (one flagged ancestor makes every descendant mesh a viewmodel; `Visibility` folds by AND). An optional `ViewmodelLens { fov_y_deg, near, depth_slice }` on the camera entity sets the projection; absent means the defaults, 55° / 0.01 / 0.01. The lens projection is independent of the gameplay FOV, `CameraOffset.fov_delta` and the late latch.
2. **Hash posture.** The gun is authored on `presentation: true` entities parented to the sim camera (`docs/presentation.md`), so the flag stays out of `WorldHash`. On a sim entity the flag is hashed like `Visibility`. `ViewmodelLens` on the sim camera is hashed; it is constant config. The flag is render-only: the sim never reads it.
3. **One depth buffer and one scene pass.** The world's depth is remapped into the rest of [0,1] only on frames that draw a viewmodel; the viewmodel gets the reserved slice. Every depth consumer — SSAO, SSR, DoF, fog, clustered lookup, transparent sort and `render.view` picks — decodes through one shared helper, so a remapped frame and an ordinary frame cannot disagree.
4. **Camera source.** The gun's camera-local transform is composed onto the frame's drawn camera at one site, a renderer `compose_viewmodel(scene, camera_model)`, called by extract, `blend` and `latch_camera` with the exact model whose inverse is the world view, so the gun cannot swim against the world. That local is `cam_entity⁻¹ · instance model`, computed in f64, so every mesh path inherits it, skins and morphs included. Its limit is about 1 ulp of the camera's world coordinates; an exact chain fold is a follow-up.
5. **Under a `render.view` override** a viewmodel draws as an ordinary world mesh.
6. **Misuse is a diagnostic, never a crash.**
   - `L1336` (warning): a `Viewmodel` mesh exists but the scene has no single camera that is both perspective and a first-person `CameraRig`. Reported once; the mesh draws as an ordinary world mesh, shadows included.
   - `L1337` (warning): a `ViewmodelLens` is on an entity without `Camera`; ignored and reported once.
   - `L1338` (error): a lens field is out of range (`fov_y_deg` finite in [1, 179], `near` finite and above 0, `depth_slice` finite in (0, 0.5]). A scene with one is refused; at runtime the defaults are used and it is reported once.
7. **Shadows.** Flagged items cast no world shadow (`cast_shadows: false` at extract). They receive a single unfiltered eye-position shadow sample, so the wall they poke into cannot darken them.
8. **Post exclusion.** SSAO, SSR and DoF skip viewmodel pixels and never sample across them; bloom, exposure, tonemap, grade and SMAA apply as for the world. Stencil versus a mask target is picked by the Planner of subtask 3, whichever the post chain samples cheapest.
9. **The flag lives on the draw item**, so skins and morphs inherit it. The skinned proof is owed to `skeletal-animation`.
10. **The remap is folded into the clip matrix, so the one decode is the frame's `inv_view_proj`.** The world's clip z becomes `z' = (1-s)·z + s·w` and the lens's `z' = s·z` (`s` = `depth_slice`), so the world occupies depth `[s, 1]` and the gun `[0, s)`. `RenderScene::depth_view_proj` is the one source of the frame's world matrix (the `Camera` uniform, the recorder's `view_proj` and its inverse, the sky), and every consumer reconstructs through that inverse, so no consumer carries a WGSL helper: recompiling the composite and shafts depth functions moves 3090 pixels. A gun pixel is told apart by `d < s` (subtask 3). Mesh batches split into world and viewmodel draws, each bound to its own camera group; decals never receive on the gun.
11. **Viewmodel lighting and exclusions.** The gun's screen position is the lens's, so the clustered tile it would read covers other space than the CPU binned its lights into, and with a lens wider than the world's the gun can sit outside the world frustum where no tile holds its lights; reprojecting the tile cannot fix that. A frame with a viewmodel therefore appends one cell after the grid (`VIEWMODEL_CELL`): every light whose bounding sphere meets the flagged meshes' union bounds, ascending, or every light when a bound is unknown. `Camera.lens.x` is 1 on the viewmodel's camera group and 0 on the world's, and gun fragments read that cell and take the sun and each local light's shadow from one sample at the camera eye (`viewmodel_local_shadow`, no normal offset, no lift, no filter). `face_scene` drops flagged meshes and the lens, and extract keeps them out of the GI voxelizer (and so dynamic injection) when they are composed. Known limit: GI sky visibility and probe irradiance still read at the gun's world position.
12. **The depth is the post mask.** SSAO, SSR and DoF skip gun pixels, told apart by `d < s` against a lane carrying the frame's slice (`0` without a viewmodel, which no stored depth is below, so such a frame is unchanged). No stencil: the depth is `Depth32Float`, which has no stencil aspect, and adding one changes the depth format of every pass and the graph. No mask target: it is an extra MRT and one more binding in every post pass, while every excluded pass already binds the depth. SSAO needs no compare: the prepass, read only by `ssao`, does not draw the gun, so the AO texture is the no-gun frame's and cannot halo, and gun fragments take `ao = 1`. SSR: the Hi-Z seed skips gun texels, the trace resolve treats them as passed-behind and an origin on the gun returns no hit, the blend gather and centre skip them. DoF: a gun texel's circle of confusion is `0` in both prefilter paths (it wins the nearest pick, scatters nowhere, and nothing behind shows through), and the composite skips the defocus mix on gun pixels so a blurred near-world foreground cannot claim them through its coverage. Bloom, exposure, tonemap, grade and SMAA are unchanged. Known limits: a world pixel whose SSR ray crosses the gun's footprint misses (the world behind the gun is not in the buffer) and falls back to probes or sky; reflections, fog, shafts and decals still decode gun pixels through the world inverse (a point near the world near plane).
13. **Viewmodel particles: the inherited marker, camera-frame simulation, and the fog evidence.** A `ParticleEmitter` whose entity is in the frame's flagged set (`Viewmodel` on it or a `Parent` ancestor) *is* a viewmodel emitter: no emitter field, no schema change, one spelling. Extract evaluates it in the first-person camera's frame (its world model composed with the camera's inverse in f64, a `CameraBasis` of identity), flags each `ParticleInstance.viewmodel`, and lists the instances in `ViewmodelData.particles` (camera-local centre, right, up); `compose_viewmodel` then rewrites them onto the drawn camera, so extract, `blend` and `latch_camera` keep the flash on the drawn muzzle with no blend-key change. `apply` returns lens data when either list is non-empty. A flagged emitter with no viewmodel camera (L1336, `render.view`) draws as a world emitter. Draw: `build_particle_draw` packs the world list then the viewmodel list, each sorted back-to-front, and the transparent pass draws the second range through a lens `Camera` group (`viewmodel_camera_bg`, the skin-less twin of decision 10's); a frame with no flagged particle packs and draws as before. The transparent layer pass draws only the world range, since a gun-layer particle must not stand in for the world behind it. Known limits: a flash writes no depth or layer, so DoF and fog over its pixels follow what lies behind; a `lit` viewmodel particle's centre shading reads the screen tile, not the viewmodel cell (flashes are additive and unlit); viewmodel particles move with the camera by construction, right for a flash and wrong for lingering smoke, which should be a world emitter. **Fog evidence (no lens decode added):** a gun pixel stores `d < s`, which the world inverse decodes to a view distance in `[0.98·near, near]` (unit test over `d` in `[0, s)`), so fog, height fog and shafts see the gun at about `near` (0.1) instead of its true 0.3 to 0.5. Measured under `fog_density` 0.5 on the M5 (unpinned): the mean gun-pixel change over the mask is 0.00 / 4.00 / 10.16 LSB (R/G/B) against a bound at `near` of 0.00 / 3.63 / 11.03 LSB (`1 − e^{−0.5·near}` through sRGB), inside bound plus 2 LSB. At authored densities `σ ≤ 0.1` the error is under 0.04 in fog fraction and invisible; a lens decode stays out unless a scene shows otherwise.

## Acceptance

- Engine goldens: the viewmodel pressed against a close wall shows no clipping; it overlapping a close enemy draws in front of it; SSAO on shows no halo around it; a muzzle-flash particle in the viewmodel layer.
- Engine tests: the flag does not change world shadows or `WorldHash`; the clustered lookup uses the true view-z, e.g. a point light behind the eye still lights the gun.
- A docs topic.
